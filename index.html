RESEARCH CONCEPT / EARLY-STAGE PROTOTYPE

Evidence-Aware, Query-Dependent Markov Ranking

Investigative relevance ranking over heterogeneous knowledge graphs.

Network importance ≠ investigative relevance.

This project explores whether Markov-based graph ranking can be extended with query relevance, evidence strength, temporal context, source trust, anomaly signals, and alternative explanations to better prioritize investigative leads.

Explore the Architecture View the Model

The Problem

Modern investigations can involve millions of records: communications, financial transactions, locations, surveillance, documents, public records, devices, vehicles, and social relationships.

Existing analytical systems can discover relationships within this information. But once a graph becomes large, a more difficult question emerges:

Which relationships deserve the investigator's attention first?

Consider a person with hundreds of connections. A conventional graph-ranking algorithm may consider that person highly important. But an investigator may instead be asking:

"Who communicated with the victim during the six hours preceding the event?"

A person with only two connections could be much more relevant if one of those connections was a direct communication with the victim shortly before the event and was supported by reliable evidence.

Core insight: Network importance is not necessarily the same thing as investigative relevance.

From Network Importance to Investigative Relevance

Traditional graph analysis often asks:

Who is important in this network?

This project explores a different question:

Given the investigator's current question, case context, and available evidence, what should be examined next?

The proposed ranking incorporates multiple signals:

Graph Structure

Network position, connectivity, and multi-hop relationships.

Query Relevance

How closely a relationship answers the investigator's current question.

Evidence Strength

Quality and corroboration of the evidence supporting a relationship.

Temporal Relevance

How closely an event occurs to the time period relevant to the investigation.

Source Trust

Reliability and authority of the source supporting the information.

Anomaly Signals

Unexpected behavior, missing relationships, or deviations from established patterns.

Why Markov Ranking?

The conceptual inspiration comes partly from PageRank and its Markov-chain interpretation of network importance.

PageRank demonstrated that the importance of a node can depend on the structure of the network around it. This project explores whether a similar graph-propagation principle can be made explicitly dependent on an investigative question.

Instead of producing a single permanent importance score, the system would produce a ranking conditioned on:

Query + Case + Evidence + Time + Trust

Changing the investigative question should therefore change the ranking.

Example

Imagine the same investigative knowledge graph is used to answer three different questions.

Question A

Who communicated with the victim during the previous 24 hours?

Communication + Time + Evidence

Question B

Who financially benefited from the victim during the previous month?

Financial + Time + Evidence

Question C

Who could have been physically present at the event?

Location + Movement + Time

The underlying graph may remain largely unchanged. The ranking should not.

Where AI Fits

The proposal does not treat an LLM as the entire reasoning engine. Different components have different responsibilities.

LLM Query understanding, extraction, explanation
Knowledge Graph Entities, relationships, evidence, temporal context
Markov Engine Graph propagation and relevance ranking
RAG Retrieval of supporting evidence
Human Investigator Interpretation, verification, and decisions

Research Question

Can a dynamically weighted Markov process over a heterogeneous investigative knowledge graph identify and prioritize entities, relationships, and evidence that are most relevant to a specific investigative question more effectively than conventional graph centrality, semantic retrieval, or LLM-based approaches operating independently?

This is an empirical question. The individual technologies involved are established areas of research. The focus of this project is their integration and measurable performance.

Current Status

Early-stage research / prototype

This project is intentionally open to criticism and prior-art discovery.

The goal is not to assume that the concept is novel, but to investigate where it overlaps with existing research and whether the proposed combination produces measurable improvements.

I'm particularly interested in:

  • Existing research I may have missed
  • Alternative mathematical formulations
  • Better evaluation methods
  • Appropriate datasets
  • Potential failure modes
  • Ways to turn the concept into a working prototype
